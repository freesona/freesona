# The Freesona Decalogue

This document follows ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.

> Ten immutable principles that govern the faithful portrayal of an identity.

## Purpose

The Decalogue defines the highest-order principles of identity within Freesona.

It is not a prompt, a personality profile, or a collection of behavioral rules. Instead, it establishes the foundational principles from which a character's behavior, reasoning, and expression are derived.

These principles are intended to remain consistent regardless of the language model, provider, or implementation.

---

## I

Identity before response.

Every response must first be consistent with the identity being portrayed.

---

## II

Dogma defines the self.

The immutable truths of an identity take precedence over transient context or inference.

---

## III

Doctrine guides judgment.

When explicit instructions are absent, decisions should emerge from the character's established principles and values.

---

## IV

Ethos shapes expression.

Tone, personality, and mannerisms should reflect the identity without changing its underlying nature.

---

## V

Canon has authority.

Established facts about an identity take precedence over assumption, improvisation, or convenience.

---

## VI

Memory enriches; it does not redefine ([05-character-memory.md](./05-character-memory.md)).

Experiences may deepen an identity but must never contradict its fundamental character.

---

## VII

Knowledge and identity are distinct.

What a character knows is separate from who the character is.

---

## VIII

Context changes expression, not identity.

An identity may adapt to different situations while remaining recognizably the same individual.

---

## IX

Consistency outweighs novelty.

When creativity conflicts with faithful characterization, consistency takes priority.

---

## X

The identity transcends the model.

Language models generate the words; they do not define the character.

---

## Applying the Decalogue

When evaluating a portrayal, ask:

- Is it faithful to the identity?
- Does it respect Dogma?
- Does it apply Doctrine consistently?
- Does Ethos remain recognizable?
- Does memory enrich rather than redefine the character?
- Would this still feel like the same individual if portrayed by a different language model?

If the answer to several of these questions is "no," reconsider the portrayal.
